# HSBC Expat: getting locked out by a device change, and how to push for a fix

*Researched 7 Oct 2026. Check names and titles on LinkedIn before you send anything. People change roles.*

The problem: the HSBC Expat app works on only one device at a time. Moving to a new phone requires a phone call, and the phone line doesn't get answered, so a Premier customer has had no access to their money for a week.

There are two separate jobs here. Keep them apart:

1. **Get your access back now.** That's a complaint, and HSBC is obliged to handle it.
2. **Get the process fixed for everyone.** That's a proposal, and HSBC is free to ignore it.

If you mix them up ("I'm locked out, and by the way, hire me"), the whole message gets sent to complaints and the proposal reads like leverage.

---

## Part 1: Get access back (do this first)

Go down this list in order and stop when one works.

1. **Email or message your Premier Relationship Manager directly.** Premier accounts usually come with a named RM who has a direct email or phone number. Look in old emails and welcome letters. This is the fastest route if you have one.
2. **Make a formal complaint, in writing, so there's a record.** A formal complaint gets a reference number, an acknowledgement deadline (5 working days) and an internal owner. Phone queues give you none of that.
   - Premier complaints line: **+44 1534 616 313** (from HSBC Expat's complaints page)
   - Post: HSBC Bank plc, Customer Complaints Team, PO Box 14, St Helier, Jersey JE4 8NJ
   - Secure message only works if you can log on. If you can still get into web banking on a computer, use it.
3. **Escalate to the Channel Islands Financial Ombudsman (CIFO)** if HSBC doesn't resolve it. HSBC Expat is HSBC Bank plc, Jersey Branch, so it falls under CIFO, not the UK Financial Ombudsman. CIFO normally expects HSBC to have had its chance first (a final response, or HSBC's own 90-day deadline passing). You can still contact CIFO now to ask how to proceed.
   - www.ci-fo.org · enquiries@ci-fo.org · +44 1534 669 800
4. **Keep a log** of each call attempt: date, time, number dialled, minutes waited, and call charges. It's your evidence for the complaint and for any compensation (redress) claim.

**Scam warning:** People who search "HSBC Expat phone number" are a known target for fake support numbers and fake "HSBC" accounts on social media. Only use numbers from expat.hsbc.com or the back of your card. Never give a passcode or Secure Key code to anyone who contacts *you*.

>

> 3. Confirm how I can make urgent payments in the meantime.
> 4. Consider redress for the loss of access and the call costs I've incurred.
>

## Part 2: Proposing the fix

### Honest expectations

- **You don't need to sell them technology. HSBC already has it.** HSBC UK's app lets customers set up a new device by scanning **photo ID and taking a selfie**, with no phone call and no waiting for a code ([HSBC UK: Photo ID and selfie](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/ways-to-bank/mobile/id-and-selfie/)). So the strongest argument isn't "here's an innovation". It's **"your own group already built this; bring it to Expat customers."** That's much harder to dismiss.
- **A cold pitch from an outside vendor to change a bank's login and security process almost never gets bought.** Changes like that go through group cybersecurity, vendor-risk checks, procurement and the regulator. Since you don't necessarily want the work, approach as a **frustrated Premier customer bringing a clear, constructive proposal**. Senior people read that kind of message, and it can land in front of the right team.
- **Your realistic upside:** a call with the HSBC Expat leadership or digital team, an invitation to a customer panel or user research session, and your case fixed fast along the way. If they then want outside help, that conversation will come from them.

### The one-pager to send (once your access is sorted, or in parallel but as a separate message)

> **Subject: A Premier customer's proposal: self-service device switching for the HSBC Expat app**
>
> Dear Ms Soldatova,
>
> I'm a Premier customer with HSBC Expat. I'm writing with a constructive proposal, not a complaint (that's being handled separately, ref [REF]).
>
> **What happened.** I changed phones. The Expat app allows one device and offers no way to switch myself, so I had to call. After [N] attempts over [X] days at different hours, nobody answered, and I couldn't access my money for [X] days. For internationally mobile customers, who lose and replace phones more than most, this will keep happening.
>
> **Why it matters to HSBC**
> - **Contact-centre cost:** every device change becomes a call that a self-service flow would avoid.
> - **Premier retention:** being unable to reach your own money is the experience most likely to make a high-value customer move banks.
> - **Conduct and resilience risk:** customers without access to funds for days because of a process gap is exactly the kind of outcome regulators look at.
>
> **The fix already exists inside HSBC.** HSBC UK customers set up a new device in the app with photo ID and a selfie, with no call needed. I'm asking for the same for Expat customers, plus:
>
> 1. **Self-service device switch** using ID document scan and a liveness selfie matched against the KYC record you already hold, with a code sent to the registered phone number or email as a second factor.
> 2. **Old device deregistered automatically**, with an alert to the registered email and phone number.
> 3. **A safety window:** on a new device, viewing and existing payees work straight away; new payees and large transfers unlock after 24–72 hours. That keeps the security benefit without locking people out.
> 4. **An optional second trusted device** (e.g. a tablet) so one lost phone doesn't mean zero access.
> 5. **A bookable call-back in the app for Premier customers** in place of an open-ended phone queue, for the cases that still need a human.
>
> **What I'm asking for:** a 30-minute call with whoever owns the Expat app roadmap. I'm happy to walk through what happened and to take part in testing or customer research.
>
> Kind regards,
> [NAME]
> HSBC Expat Premier customer since [YEAR] · [LinkedIn URL]

### Sequence

1. **Today:** start Part 1 (RM email and written complaint). Start the call log.
2. **Same week:** send the one-pager on LinkedIn to Anastasia Soldatova, CC Rachel Sault by email if you can find a verified address. Keep it a separate message from the complaint.
3. **+2 weeks with no reply:** send it once, by post, to Warwick Long's office in Jersey, quoting your complaint reference.
4. **Complaint unresolved by HSBC's deadline:** go to CIFO.

Optional and use with care: a calm, factual public post (no account details) tagging HSBC can speed up a stuck complaint. Do it *after* the written complaint exists, and never post anything that identifies your account.
