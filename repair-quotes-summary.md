**$0** if you do it yourself, or **up to ~$50** at a shop [Estimate]. If a panel shop is already working on the quarter panel, the lamp has to come out anyway, so fitting should cost little or nothing extra.

**Write-off?**
- At independent or mobile prices, **no**. The car is clearly worth repairing.
- If RAC's repairer quotes about **$2,300 or more**, RAC may treat it as an economic write-off.
- If RAC writes it off, the number that matters to you is the car's **pre-accident market value minus its salvage value**, not the repair quote.
- As a general rule (not legal advice), the at-fault party pays the *lesser* of the reasonable repair cost and the loss in the car's value. A demand that's higher than the car is worth is worth challenging.

### Using these numbers with RAC or the owner
- Ask RAC for an **itemised** quote (parts, labour hours, paint and materials), the assessor's photos, and, if it's a write-off, the market value and salvage figures they used.
- Check the quote **excludes** the boot lid, the rear bumper scuffs and general wear.
- **Query any quarter-panel replacement.** For a crease, repairing the panel is normal [Estimate].
- Ask RAC to justify any towing, hire-car or assessment fees.
- **If you settle directly with the owner:**
  - Get written confirmation that they haven't lodged an RAC claim, or that it's been withdrawn.
  - Get a signed release: "full and final settlement of all damage from the incident on 30/09/2026".
  - If RAC has already paid the owner, paying the owner as well won't stop RAC claiming the money from you.
